# Closing: States and Transitions

*A practical method for shaping the states we leave behind*

This exploratory note describes a method of closing developed from personal experience. It examines the states left by everyday actions, tasks, projects, and personal experiences, and how those states influence what follows.

The method leaves the definition of a good state to the person applying it. It provides a working perspective and practical questions rather than a general theory of correct decisions or a good life.

A later correction may reveal that an earlier claim was wrong. Retaining that version makes the development and the grounds for revision easier to examine.

## Scope

The note focuses on the states that remain when an activity ends or pauses. It addresses:

- everyday actions and shared processes;
- tasks, partial results, and larger projects;
- deliberate openness and scheduled continuation;
- carrying experience into later action;
- and feedback about the usefulness of resulting states.

It does not prescribe personal life goals, define universally good states, or claim established effectiveness across these domains.

## Development

The note began with a discussion of personal endings and the experience retained after success, failure, or loss. Its focus broadened to everyday actions, recognizable household states, partial project results, and deliberate openness.

The central clarification was to focus on the state left behind rather than on an exhaustive classification of endings. The creation of the first stable version became one of the note's own examples.
