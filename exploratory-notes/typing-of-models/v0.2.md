# On the Typing of Models
### Orientation, Explanation, Application, and Meta Models

**Model type:** Meta Model  
**Scope:** Analytical models across scientific, organizational, and practical contexts.  
**Status:** Exploratory Note  
**Version:** v0.2  
**Last updated:** 2026-08-02

---

## Abstract

This exploratory note proposes a minimal functional typology of analytical models.

It argues that many disagreements about models do not arise from their content, but from unspoken assumptions about **what a model is meant to do**.

By distinguishing between **orientation models**, **explanatory models**, **application models**, and **meta models**, the note provides a functional vocabulary for describing how models are intended to be used.

---

## Scope and Intent

This note classifies models by their **primary function**, not by their truth, precision, or scientific status.

The typology is intended as a tool for conceptual orientation: to clarify how a model is used, what kind of questions it can answer, and which expectations are structurally misplaced.

It does not propose a general theory of modeling, nor does it prescribe how models should be constructed.

---

## Starting Point

Models are ubiquitous across science, engineering, management, and everyday reasoning.

They are often discussed as if they differed only in accuracy or abstraction. However, many disagreements arise because models with **different functions** are treated as if they were interchangeable.

The decisive question is therefore not:

> Is the model correct?

but rather:

> What is this model meant to do?

---

## The Problem of Functional Ambiguity

In practice, models are frequently used outside the function for which they were constructed.

Orientation models are treated as action guides.

Explanatory models are mistaken for operational tools.

Application models are expected to provide conceptual clarity.

Meta models are overlooked entirely.

This functional ambiguity leads to recurring problems:

- misplaced criticism
- false expectations of precision or completeness
- confusion between understanding and action
- escalation from conceptual disagreement to normative conflict

The issue is not the model itself, but the absence of functional typing.

---

## A Minimal Typology of Models

To reduce this ambiguity, a minimal functional classification can be introduced.

The typology distinguishes between four complementary model types:

- Orientation Models
- Explanatory Models
- Application Models
- Meta Models

These categories describe **function**, not quality.

A model may be excellent within its intended function and misleading outside it.

---

## Orientation Models

Orientation models provide **situational understanding**.

They answer questions such as:

- Where are we?
- What kind of situation is this?
- Which dynamics are structurally present?

Characteristics:

- non-prescriptive
- stabilizing
- low operational resolution
- resistant to narrow falsification

Orientation models do not tell actors what to do.

They reduce the risk of acting under false assumptions.

---

## Explanatory Models

Explanatory models provide **causal or structural understanding**.

They answer questions such as:

- Why did this happen?
- Which mechanisms are involved?
- How are different elements connected?

Characteristics:

- retrospective strength
- causal or relational structure
- medium abstraction
- evaluated by explanatory coherence

Explanatory models primarily support understanding rather than intervention.

---

## Application Models

Application models support **concrete action and decision-making**.

They answer questions such as:

- What should be done?
- How should a process be designed?
- Which rules or steps should be followed?

Characteristics:

- operational
- context-sensitive
- risk-exposed
- evaluated by practical effectiveness

Application models require explicit assumptions and clearly defined boundaries.

---

## Meta Models

Meta models describe, classify, compare, or constrain other models rather than directly representing aspects of reality.

They answer questions such as:

- What kind of model is this?
- What is its intended function?
- Under which conditions is a model appropriate?
- How do different models relate to each other?

Characteristics:

- model-oriented rather than reality-oriented
- conceptual rather than operational
- provides analytical vocabulary
- organizes other models without replacing them

Meta models improve analytical clarity by making assumptions about models explicit.

---

## Relations Between Model Types

The four model types are complementary rather than hierarchical.

Meta Models organize and classify the remaining model types.

Orientation Models support situational understanding.

Explanatory Models support structural understanding.

Application Models support action under defined conditions.

No model type can fully substitute for another without loss.

---

## Typical Misuses

Several recurring errors arise from ignoring model function:

- orientation models are criticized for lacking actionable detail
- explanatory models are expected to produce decisions
- application models are treated as universal truths
- meta models are mistaken for explanations of reality

These are functional rather than substantive errors.

---

## Methodological Implication

A minimal rule follows:

> Before evaluating a model, its primary function should be made explicit.

A common analytical sequence is:

1. Clarify the model type (Meta Model).
2. Understand the situation (Orientation Model).
3. Explain the relevant mechanisms (Explanatory Model).
4. Support action where required (Application Model).

Not every analysis requires every step.

---

## Known Limitations

This typology classifies models by their primary function.

It does not address:

- model quality
- empirical validity
- mathematical formalism
- levels of abstraction
- discipline-specific classifications

The categories are intended as analytical tools rather than exhaustive classifications.

---

## Core Statement

Many disagreements about models are not disagreements about reality, but about **what kind of work the model is expected to perform**.

Making model function explicit resolves such disagreements without requiring additional data or methodological refinement.
