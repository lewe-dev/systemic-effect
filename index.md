# Systemic Effect

**A changing map for thinking about systems, decisions, and effects.**

Decisions do not begin with action.

Before action, there are expectations.  
Behind expectations are models.  
Models depend on assumptions, observations, conditions, goals, and the way we interpret them.

Failures can therefore occur long before execution fails.

Systemic Effect explores this space.

---

## A current map

The notes currently tend to converge around several recurring questions:

### Orientation

What are we actually trying to achieve?

Are we solving the right problem — or merely becoming better at solving the one we have defined?

### Models & Expectations

How well does our understanding represent the relevant reality?

What should we expect before acting, and what would make us revise that expectation?

### Conditions & Possibilities

Which structures and conditions determine what is possible?

Which possibilities exist only under particular conditions — and what changes when those conditions change?

### Action & Behavior

How do rules, incentives, constraints, understanding, and context translate into behavior?

Why can the same rule or intervention produce different effects under different conditions?

### Effects & Measurement

What actually changed?

Are we measuring the effect that matters, or only what is easiest to observe?

### Feedback & Adaptation

Can reality correct the models, rules, and expectations that produced the action?

What happens when feedback is missing, delayed, distorted, or ignored?

---

These areas are not separate stages, and they do not form a fixed framework.

They overlap, interact, and can change one another.

**This map is provisional. Like every city map, it is likely already slightly out of date.**

Categories may move, merge, disappear, or be replaced as the collection develops.

The map describes the current structure of the collection. It does not define what the collection must become.

**[Working notes](/working-notes/)** contain models that have reached sufficient stability to serve as reference points.
